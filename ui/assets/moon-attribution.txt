AstroApex interactive lunar terrain

NASA Scientific Visualization Studio, CGI Moon Kit, Ernie Wright
https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4720/

Surface imagery: NASA's 2025 rendering mosaic from the Lunar Reconnaissance
Orbiter Camera Wide Angle Camera (LROC WAC), with lower-resolution LOLA laser
albedo poleward of 70 degrees. NASA adjusted exposure and filled data gaps.
8,192 x 4,096 sRGB TIFF:
https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/vis/a000000/a004700/a004720/lroc_color_16bit_srgb_8k.tif
Original 2,048 x 1,024 preview (moon-color.jpg):
https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/vis/a000000/a004700/a004720/lroc_color_2k.jpg

Terrain: Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter Lunar Orbiter Laser Altimeter (LOLA)
global cylindrical DEM, 5,760 x 2,880 (16 samples per degree).
https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/vis/a000000/a004700/a004720/ldem_16_uint.tif
Grid heights in kilometers above 1,737.4 km = (unsigned value - 20,000)/2,000.

Web derivatives: 512 longitude sectors and 256 latitude intervals. Every vertex
uses bilinearly resampled source elevation and true radial displacement. Seam
vertices coincide and pole elevations are averaged per polar row. Local normals
are east/north/up surface gradients calculated from the native DEM using
latitude-dependent pixel spacing. The renderer strengthens the tangent slope
components by 1.75 for readability in relief mode. This fixed display lighting
is independent of mission date. Uniform lighting instead shows surface albedo.

Global imagery and normal previews use 4,096 x 2,048 or 2,048 x 1,024 samples.
Close-up tiles use the original 8,192-wide color and 5,760-wide elevation-normal
resolutions. Up to four 45 x 45 degree tiles are retained on the GPU, with two
source pixels of edge padding. Fine polar image detail is mostly elevation
shading. Linear detail varies with latitude and sampling; no local hazard or
engineering measurement is implied by these global products.

Longitude increases east; the image top is lunar north. The prime meridian is
centered. In the model, +Y is north; +Z points to (0 N, 0 E); +X to (0 N, 90 E).
The scale bar and click coordinates use the 1,737.4 km reference sphere.
