NASA/JPL Horizons DE441 — apparent lunar sky geometry

Coverage: 2026-01-01 00:00 UTC through 2027-12-31 23:00 UTC, inclusive.
17,520 unique hourly samples, Sun and Earth, in the Horizons lunar MEAN_ME frame.
Reference sphere = 1737.4 km, reference altitude = 0 km, east-positive longitude.
Source and API documentation: https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons/manual.html
https://ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov/doc/horizons.html

Method: queried Horizons observer quantities 4 and 20 (airless apparent azimuth
and elevation in degrees, apparent range in km) as viewed from lunar 0 N, 0 E.
Each row becomes an approximate apparent Cartesian position relative to the Moon's
center: X = R + range*sin(el); Y = range*cos(el)*sin(az); Z = range*cos(el)*cos(az).
Axes point to the prime meridian, 90 degrees east, and the north pole respectively.
Components are rounded to 0.001 km. For a chosen site, subtract its spherical
Cartesian position then project onto local east, north and up. This accounts
for observer parallax. Light-time, aberration, and gravitational light bending
are inherited from the reference observer; small location-specific differences
remain. Epochs fall on whole UTC hours. Event times falling between samples are
bracketed. The angle series alone does not model terrain, solar-disk clearance,
panel orientation, eclipses or a communications link budget.

Validation: compared all 336 hourly samples for Sun and Earth at each of the three
preset mission sites against their original JPL observer tables (Oct 18–31, 2026).
Additional direct queries sample 25 consecutive hours in other regions and months.
