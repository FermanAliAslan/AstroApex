Derivation
At each fixed coordinate, take the bilinear DEM height and add 2 m radially.
For bearings 0–359.5 degrees (north=0, east=90), trace a spherical great
circle out to 180 km. Sample the 80 m DEM bilinearly every 5 m up to 2 km,
then every 40 m. For a sample at angular separation g, surface radius r,
and observer radius r0, geometric elevation is atan2(r*cos(g)-r0,r*sin(g)).
The skyline is the maximum along each bearing. Preserve negative skyline
elevations on ridges. Round to 0.001 degrees for compact storage and interpolate
between adjacent bearings (including 359.5 to 0) at runtime.

Use and uncertainty
The Sun or Earth center clears terrain when its elevation exceeds the skyline
at its own azimuth. Hourly sampling limits transition timing. This is a model
for a single 2 m observer at each named coordinate; it does not apply across
the whole region. Features smaller than a DEM cell, angular sampling, terrain
beyond the 180 km search radius, terrain uncertainty and observing height can
change the results. Visibility does not model partial disks, solar panel output,
antenna coverage or link budget. A height of zero relative to the lunar
reference sphere is used for the printed Horizons comparison references;
the terrain observer height is relative to actual local ground.

The landing-site picker thumbnails (site-reliefs.png) are shaded-relief views
of 28 km squares centered on the three sites in the same 80 m LOLA raster.
Lighting and violet tint are illustrative.

The mission planner's site maps (plan-relief.webp) use local, north-up
56 km by 28 km shaded-relief crops centered at the same three coordinates.
Their fixed map lighting illustrates topography. Visibility at the selected
UTC hour is calculated separately from the ephemeris and active horizon model.
